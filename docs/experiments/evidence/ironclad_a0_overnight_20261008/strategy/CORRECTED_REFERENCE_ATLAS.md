# Corrected-reference loss and decision atlas

This is an outcome-unselected, descriptive review of the completed wave-2 **Fprod** reference. It contains all 128 endpoints and a chronological review of exactly the first twelve source seeds, `610082000..610082011`, including wins and losses. The review is **not blinded**. It does not select new policy parameters, pool cohorts, train a model, or open final confirmation. Unusual decisions remain unresolved unless source or recorded action evidence establishes a narrower mechanism.

The corrected reference won **101/128 (78.90625%)**. Its 27 deaths occurred in Act 1 three times, Act 2 eight times, and Act 3 sixteen times. The first twelve sources won nine times; their three deaths are different enough that a single campfire explanation is not supported.

The public state at T0:331 has69/72HP, a Speed Potion, Mark of Pain/Fusion Hammer, and a29-card deck including Reaper, Limit Break and Spot Weakness. V4 takes bits0. R0:959 starts the fight at71HP after battle-init effects; R0:984 ends the recorded roots at turn4,18HP,34 total enemy HP, with only one legal action. T0:332 records death. This proves a chosen optional exposure and the subsequent loss. It does not establish why the combat was lost, the success probability of entering, or the full-run value of leaving. No concrete hand/intents/action alternatives were serialized for this cohort.

**HP trade and attrition, source002.** The Altar transition T0:420 is an identifiable earlier resource decision:87/87→70/92. It is followed by several recorded losses of HP, not an unexplained last-floor collapse. T0:452 then adds27HP before the boss. R0:1235 begins Automaton at60HP; the last root R0:1276 is turn5 at38HP with19 enemy HP and only one legal action. A small enemy remainder is not proof that another card order or potion would win: the cards and legal alternatives are missing. Runic Dome, two Offerings, the attack-heavy deck, and earlier route/upgrade decisions are competing explanations, not isolated causes. The chronology cannot reconstruct alternative map paths either.

**Brimstone and near-full entry, source006.** The earliest specific strength-race decision is the f19 V4 Brimstone purchase, made at88/88HP with365 gold and public alternative purchases. The record later shows both Act2 survival and Act3 progress. At f49, the actual choice is smith at94/100, not a full-health REST. R1:1012 begins Awakened One at94HP; R1:1041 is turn5 at7HP with32 total enemy HP and two legal actions, chosen bits2147483648 (end turn). This is a locator for unresolved combat diagnosis, not a claim that ending turn was dominated: the second action, hand, intent, remaining boss phases and attainable damage/block are unavailable. Two retained Forge potions alone do not close those gaps. Source005's Brimstone/Reaper win at full HP is retained as a contrary mechanistic example, without treating different seeds as causal pairs.

## Mechanisms, benign controls, and unresolved explanations

The 98 campfire menus include20 full-health roots. Eleven open a smith selector; one full-health Dream Catcher rest opens a reward; one Fusion Hammer state has only REST after optional Recall filtering. The remaining **seven full-health RESTs with legal Smith and no Dream Catcher** are in sources004,007,009. All seven are V4-owned, and all three runs won. This confirms the already identified choice mechanism survives the corrected reference. It does not identify its regret, prove upgrade dominance, or justify tuning a rule on these outcomes. Upgrade interactions such as Limit Break losing exhaust and changed V4 margins remain material counterexamples to a deterministic-dominance argument.

Exactly one of the98 recorded REST menus still contains Recall, and it contains no other action: source008 atf41. This is the declared forced-action exception to `STS_OOC_SKIP_OPTIONAL_RECALL`, not a return of the old optional random key detour. The subsequent only-skip menus and source011's only-REST menu are explicit benign controls against classifying unchanged inventory as a policy mistake.

The removal observations have a separate, source-supported ownership explanation. In frozen3ed `runFullRunOocPolicyStep`, native `removeSelectVPolicy` is selected only when `curRoom == SHOP`. Event REMOVE screens reach `ScumSearchAgent2::cardSelectPolicy`. That function assigns **negative obtain weight** to REMOVE and sorts ascending, favoring cards with high acquisition weights. `Expert::getObtainWeight` assigns Offering65, Shrug15 and Strike/Defend0. This matches the newly obtained Offering/Shrug removals in sources011/010. Offering can tie other65-weight cards; this observation does not establish a unique intended tie-break. The same code arm covers BONFIRE_SPIRITS, where sacrificing a valuable card has a different reward mechanism; a hypothetical repair must not silently apply one interpretation to both. This audit makes no implementation change and no win-rate claim. Source008's early Anger sacrifice is a different select type and visibly heals5HP; it is not automatically another erroneous removal.

The already reproduced native shop deck-size defect remains present in Fprod because its flag is off. This atlas does **not** attribute every unusual shop choice to that defect. In particular, the Brimstone purchases above are V4-owned and bypass the declined-gate native bundle ranking that contains the arithmetic issue. Concrete owner attribution matters more than the superficial appearance of a surprising purchase.

No model score gap in this packet is treated as a calibrated probability or regret estimate. No retained potion, low HP ending, large deck, newly removed card, or high-margin loss is by itself labelled wrong. The trace supports precise observed transitions and some policy owners; full information-set combat alternatives and counterfactual continuations remain absent for these cases.

## What the negative RestOff development result changes

The completed, independently reviewed wave-2 report gives Fprod101 wins and RestOff97 on the same128 fresh source seeds: RestOff minus Fprod is−3.125 percentage points, with its reported95% paired-bootstrap interval−10.15625 to+4.6875 points. This does not prove harm, but it fails the frozen advancement rule and fails to repeat the earlier32-source improvement. No pooling with the earlier cohort is appropriate, especially because the corrected reference includes the optional Recall eligibility change.

RestOff disables the learned REST choice **and** the separate upgrade-target gate; its fallback still uses HP thresholds and random action selection in the middle HP band. It therefore changes much more than the seven local full-health rests identified here. The corrected chronology includes useful healing, low-HP smiths, beneficial Dream Catcher rewards, forced actions, and two independent removal owners. The negative broad ablation weakens the blanket explanation that removing the learned REST subsystem raises whole-run win probability. It neither proves every current REST choice good nor establishes the separately preregistered FullHp candidate. This atlas supplies baseline context only; it does not adapt that candidate, reassign seeds, or consume confirmation evidence.

## Reproduction and durable impact

The helper reads fixed `wave2/Fprod_*` files only, verifies receipts, decodes encounter IDs using `include/constants/MonsterEncounters.h`, and writes a new atlas with refuse-overwrite semantics. Reproduction is a pure evidence transformation, not a seed-driving command. The JSON includes every raw input locator/hash needed to inspect the aggregate or a named chronology. The existing independent `audit/wave2-interpretation.md` and `wave2_report.json` are the sources for the RestOff comparison; no other-arm per-seed trajectories were inspected for this atlas.

Knowledge impact: experimental evidence only. This report updates the corrected-reference baseline audit and records the boundary of the inherited event-removal behavior. It does not promote a default, declare a champion, or replace the maintained behavior contract without a separately reviewed change.
